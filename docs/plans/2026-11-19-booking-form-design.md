## Words (Nov 22 2026)

The copywriter pass (Hannah: "It's just too wordy. We don't need to market everything. It needs to be clear. Less is more."). The rules, from the fetched pages (GOV.UK, [question pages](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/question-pages/): the heading covers only what the page asks, ask for each thing once, hint text only for what helps most users and one short sentence; NN/g, [How Users Read on the Web](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-users-read-on-the-web/): most visitors scan, and a version with about half the words scored 58% better on usability in their study; NN/g, [Placeholders in Form Fields Are Harmful](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/form-design-placeholders/): keep labels outside the field and essential hints visible): a string stays only if it carries a **fact** or an **instruction**. No marketing, no reassurance, no explanation a customer does not need, no promise. A heading is the plain name of what the step asks. **The price and when it is charged are said once**, in the open service card; the card step says when the saved card is charged because that is how the saved card is used; nothing else repeats it. NN/g does not test reassurance text on forms specifically; that application is mine.

## One catalogue, built on the backend rows

`src/lib/booking/services.ts` builds the booking catalogue from the backend's `SERVICE_LIST` (`src/lib/services.ts`, the rows of the `service` table). Each booking entry names the backend keys behind it (`serviceKeys`) and **throws if one is missing**, so the form cannot offer a service the backend does not have. Price, length and what a visit includes are read from those rows. Every entry carries `online`, the same three as Settings > Services' Online select: `bookable` (Book a day), `quote` (We quote), `off`. It is worked out from the rows (`bookable`, `quoteOnly`, `onlineOfRow`) unless Settings says otherwise, and `bookingCatalogue({ repair: "off" })` is what turning a service Off looks like: the booking list is the bookable entries (`bookableOf`), the request path lists the quote ones (`quotableOf`), and what is off appears nowhere. The story **Service turned off in Settings** flips Sprinkler repair through Book a day, We quote and Off with a control and shows the card appear, move to Need a quote and disappear.

## Day and time (Nov 22 2026)

Hannah: "I don't like the way that you're surfacing times. We should always try to get them in the soonest. This is an e-commerce booking flow. Go research best practices ... It should probably be just a calendar that they can pick from. Make it really easy. We can try to optimize days later; maybe we highlight recommended ones."

**What I fetched on Nov 22 2026.** Calendly's [new scheduling page](https://calendly.com/blog/new-scheduling-page-ui): a full month view with availability on the same screen, day and time on one page (it does not say it preselects a day). Jobber's [online booking help](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/online-booking/): available days highlighted, the chosen day darker, the times below the calendar, and an "earliest availability" setting for how soon after submitting a client can book. NN/g, [Date-Input Form Fields](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/date-input/): unavailable dates "grayed out and disabled" or left out, block illogical choices visibly, label today, lists get tedious past about ten options (the argument for a calendar over a list of days). Smashing Magazine, [Designing The Perfect Date And Time Picker](https://smashingmagazine.com/2017/07/designing-perfect-date-time-picker): prefill only when the user is likely to choose that value, with time-sensitive bookings as the allowed case; showing availability inside the picker avoids a zero-results page; a time field that appears after the date is the simplest approach; month steppers become tedious after about ten taps. W3C WAI-ARIA Authoring Practices, [Date Picker Dialog](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/dialog-modal/examples/datepicker-dialog/): `role="grid"` with a name, roving tabindex so one day is in the Tab order, arrows by day and week, Page Up and Page Down by month, Home and End to the week's ends, Enter or Space picks, `aria-selected` on the chosen day, an `aria-live` month heading. W3C, [Understanding SC 2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html): WCAG 2.2 requires at least 24 by 24 CSS pixels.

**Unverified or not found.** Baymard's date-picker and time-booking pages are paywalled; a search summary says unclear availability costs people effort and that a clear visual hierarchy was the best fix for time slots (UNVERIFIED, summary only). Square Appointments' help pages fetched had nothing on how a client picks a day. Apple's 44 point figure and WCAG's 44 pixel enhanced target (2.5.5) could not be confirmed from a fetched page; we use 44 because the form already does. **No source found for marking recommended days**; that is my design and says so.

The eight rules:

1. **A month calendar, then the times of the chosen day.** One screen, availability visible, times below (Calendly, Jobber, Smashing). The list of three day cards and "Show more days" are gone.
2. **The soonest open day is selected and marked.** The form always tries to get them in soonest (Hannah). The first open day starts selected and is marked "Soonest" in its name and the legend; its times show at once, and a time is never selected for the customer, so the first thing to choose is a time. (Smashing: prefill is right in a time-sensitive booking.)
3. **Days with nothing open are greyed and disabled, never hidden** (NN/g). A greyed day is still in the grid and in the reading order, named "no times open".
4. **Recommended days carry a quiet mark.** The days the offer rule ranks best fit (`best` on the offer) get a dot and the word "Recommended" in the legend and in the day's name. They are not selected, and nothing says why (no "we're already in Erie"). Marking is my design.
5. **Times are soonest first, "Best fit" marked on the one the rule gives**, none selected, all shown (no "More times").
6. **The month arrows replace "Show more days"**, bounded to the months that have an open day, with the month in a live heading; a service with dates is offered through its next run of dates and no further (the engine design), so the range is short.
7. **A real grid.** `role="grid"`, one day in the Tab order, the APG keys, 44px day buttons each named with weekday, date and marks.
8. **A full calendar says so and still lets the customer move on.** "These days are full. We'll call you to find one." with a box for when would suit and Ryan's number; Continue stays on.

The calendar is `DayCalendar` (Blocks / DayCalendar); the stand-in days are `fixtureCalendarDays` (every working day to the window's close, about one in five full, some marked best). The live screen reads the same shape from `offerFor`; `best` on each offer is the rule's ranking, and the rule marks one day in its top five, so the live calendar will show fewer recommended days than the stand-in.

## Address and ZIP (Nov 22 2026)

Hannah: "I need to make sure that we are validating the city by ZIP code. That's our region. I want to make sure there's type ahead, and on the back end we're going to be setting up a Google API or something to do type ahead on their address."

**What I fetched on Nov 22 2026.** Baymard, [Automatic Address Lookup](https://baymard.com/research-articles/automatic-address-lookup): live suggestions that fill the other fields worked best, 9% of manually typed shipping addresses had typos, **but Baymard says to keep street, city, state and ZIP fields visible at all times, and 69% of participants whose address was not suggested completed it by hand**. Baymard, [ZIP Code Auto-Detection](https://baymard.com/research-articles/zip-code-auto-detection): detect city and state as soon as the fifth digit is typed and let the user correct them. GOV.UK, [Addresses](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/addresses/): always provide a manual option for an address that is missing, with specific error messages. Google, [Place Autocomplete (New)](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/places/web-service/place-autocomplete): suggestions come as `placePrediction` objects with a `placeId`, and a session token groups the typing and the Place Details call (the live provider sends the token; the page does not say exactly when a session ends). I found **no source on validating a service area by ZIP and no wording for a city that disagrees with its ZIP**; the mismatch line is mine.

**Decisions.**

1. **The box is the type-ahead.** Suggestions as you type from a `PlaceProvider`: the fixture provider now, `googlePlaces` behind `/api/places` later. That seam is unchanged. "Type it instead" is deleted everywhere (the booking form and the Season Pass checkout share the one address step).
2. **Typed fields only after a search finds nothing.** A query of five characters with no match opens "We can't find it. Enter it here." with Street address, City and ZIP, the street filled in. **Baymard's finding runs the other way** (keep the fields visible); Hannah's rule is what is built, and it is a question below.
3. **The ZIP decides.** `serviceability` reads the ZIP first: a ZIP in `SERVICE_ZIPS` is served and gives the town, any other five digit ZIP is outside whatever the city says. Only an address with no ZIP yet (a Google suggestion before its details answer) falls back to the city name, for the moment the details take. **`SERVICE_ZIPS` is still the unconfirmed list** (80516 Erie; 80501, 80503, 80504 Longmont; 80027 Louisville; 80026 Lafayette; 80520 Firestone; 80020, 80021, 80023 Broomfield): Ryan must confirm it before go-live.
4. **ZIP is required in the typed fallback**, five digits ("Add your ZIP code.", "A ZIP code is five digits.").
5. **The city is checked against the ZIP, quietly.** A typed city that is not the town of its ZIP shows "ZIP 80516 is in Erie." under the city and Continue does not pass; a picked address is never checked, because the provider's own city is not ours to second-guess. Outside the area the old line stands, with the six cities and Ryan's number.

Shopify Help, [Customer accounts](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/customers/customer-accounts): an email, a single-use 6 digit code, no password, saved details filled in at checkout once signed in. OWASP, [Authentication Cheat Sheet](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Authentication_Cheat_Sheet.html): login, reset and registration should answer the same way whether or not an account exists, so an attacker cannot learn who is a customer; it notes generic messages can confuse real users. Clerk, [sign-up and sign-in options](https://clerk.com/docs/guides/configure/auth-strategies/sign-up-sign-in-options): email codes and links expire after 10 minutes, a link must be opened in the same browser where sign-in began. Baymard, [Make Guest Checkout the Most Prominent Option](https://baymard.com/research-articles/make-guest-checkout-prominent): a sign-in invitation that appears only after the email is typed was mostly overlooked, an email that matches an account should still be able to continue as a guest, and 24% of a 4,384 person survey abandoned a cart in a quarter solely because an account was forced. NN/g, [Login Walls](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/login-walls/): recovering a forgotten password is tedious; offer an account after the purchase. ICO, [Data minimisation](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/data-protection-principles/a-guide-to-the-data-protection-principles/data-minimisation/): data must be adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary (UK GDPR, not CCPA). Stripe, [save and reuse](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/save-and-reuse): the customer opts in to keeping a payment method, with a checkbox.

**Unverified or not found.** Jobber's online booking page says nothing about how an existing client's details are handled (a forum summary says owners tell clients to sign into Client Hub first; that is a workaround, UNVERIFIED). Square's pages fetched covered only staff-side lookup. Capturing a form's values before it is submitted has no regulator page in what I fetched; the "Leaky Forms" study pages returned 404 (UNVERIFIED). Showing only a saved card's brand and last four is what the form does, but the Stripe page's card fields were collapsed, so I could not confirm it from the page. No fetched source words "Not you?" or covers prefill with edit.

**Decision.**

- **What we keep.** For a customer: name, email, phone, the way we reach them, the properties and the card on file (brand and last four are all the form ever shows). For a visitor who leaves: **nothing**. The match at the contact step is a lookup that stores nothing, and the link is sent only when the visitor presses a button. (The form's own rule since Nov 20: nothing is kept before the contact step is submitted.)
- **Recognise at the contact step.** Her email or phone, once typed in full, matches. The form then shows one block above the fields: "Booked with us before? Get a link to use your saved details." with **Text me a link** and **Email me a link**, and under them **Continue without signing in**, so it stays a guest booking (Baymard: the offer is visible at the top of the step, not injected after the fact, and a match may still be a guest).
- **Verify before anything shows.** Until the link is opened the visitor sees only that offer and, once sent, where it went with the digits and letters hidden, "We sent a link to (•••) •••-0123." No name, no address, no card, no "Welcome back" (the stories assert it). A link, not a code, because Hannah asked for a link by text or email; Clerk's same-browser limit means a link opened on another device fails, so "Send it again" is always there, and a code is the fallback to consider.
- **Welcome back.** The link opened: "Welcome back, Andrea." with the contact fields, the way we reach her and the card filled in and still editable; under it the address of this booking marked "On file" (or "New, we'll add it" when it is not one of hers) and the card, each with Change. The card step shows "Visa ending 4242" with "Use a different card". **Not you?** empties what was filled in and the form carries on as a new customer.
- **The trade-off, stated.** Offering the link only when something matches tells a visitor that the email or phone is a customer's, which OWASP says to avoid and Stripe Link accepts. The offer is the only thing it reveals, it needs a typed email or phone (no browsing), and the server must rate-limit the lookup and answer every visitor in the same time. The alternative with no leak is a "Have an account? Sign in" line shown to everyone and a link that says the same thing whether or not the email is on file; it is a question for Hannah.
- **Not in this pass.** Recognising someone before the address step so a saved property can be picked (the Amazon pattern, and what a returning customer with two properties needs); the account's own sign-in is the other designer's.

Stories: Contact, we know this email; Contact, link sent; Contact, welcome back; Contact, not you; Contact, continue without signing in. A new customer is the plain Contact story.

## Navigation and close

Research, Nov 20 2026. Fetched pages are cited; anything I could not fetch is marked unverified.

- **NN/g, [progress indicators](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progress-indicators/)** (fetched): show the number of steps rather than only a percentage, pair it with words, give it enough prominence, and for longer processes let people stop.
- **NN/g, [wizards](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/wizards/)** (fetched): include explicit buttons for the next and the previous step; show the steps and which one is current; a wizard is not gracefully interruptible, so quitting midway can lose work, and saving progress matters for long or central processes, not for simple ones. NN/g's multi-step forms article returned 404 and is not cited.
- **Jobber, [online booking](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/online-booking/)** (fetched; their form checks a zip first, ours checks the address): zip check first, then services with price and duration, contact and address, a calendar and a time, then Confirm Booking; a Back button returns to the previous screen; the page describes no exit or cancel control; a confirmation screen and an email follow.
- **GOV.UK Design System, [back link](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/back-link/)** and **[error summary](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/error-summary/)** (fetched): the back link takes people to the previous page in the state they last saw it, because the browser's Back button can break journeys; the error summary sits at the top, above the h1, moves focus to itself, links each message to its field (the first one when several) and uses the same words as beside the field.
- **WCAG 2.2, [page titled](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/page-titled.html)** and **[focus order](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-order.html)** (fetched): a single-page app should change its title with the view, and changed content must keep a meaningful focus order.
- **MDN, [pushState](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/History/pushState)** (fetched): each pushState is a history entry, and Back fires `popstate` with that entry's state.
- **Unverified.** Square Appointments' flow (the support page fetched was the help-centre home with no booking content), Calendly's invitee flow (help pages redirected; a search found only host-side rescheduling notes), OpenTable (only third-party critiques, which say it moves straight to a final reservation screen after a time is picked), Airbnb (only forum and help-centre notes: you can step through the request flow without sending, and nothing documents its back or close controls) and Typeform (its help page returned 403; community posts say its back arrows are hard to see and its progress bar can mislead with branching). None of these is used as evidence below.

The rules adopted:

1. **The logo links back to the site, and Close (an X, top right) is on every step.** It is a link to the website, so leaving is always one tap and works without JavaScript. On the booked screen it is still there. (Jobber's page names no exit; NN/g says people need a way to stop.)
2. **Close asks only once something is entered.** Before that it just leaves. After, it opens "Leave without booking? Your answers aren't saved." with Keep booking and Leave. Nothing is saved before the contact step, and the dialog says so truthfully.
3. **Back is a visible secondary button beside Continue, and always works.** It is never a small link at the far edge; it is 48px, outlined, left of Continue, and absent only on step 1 (where the logo and Close are the way out). NN/g asks for explicit previous and next buttons.
4. **Back and the browser's Back are the same thing.** Every step is a browser-history entry (`pushState` with the step as state; `popstate` restores it), so a phone's Back button goes to the previous step and never out of the form, with the answers kept; Forward works; the Back button calls `history.back()`. After booking, Back is ignored so Confirm cannot book twice. (GOV.UK: take people back to the state they last saw.)
5. **The progress is a real bar with words: "Step 3 of 6".** In the card, above the question, not fixed to the top of the screen. The count is the steps the path has, so a request for a quote reads "of 4". (NN/g: step counts, with prominence.)
6. **Enter submits a step** from a one-line field or a chosen card (not from a textarea, a button, a box or the address list, where Enter already means something).
7. **Focus moves to the step's heading on every change, and the page title follows** ("Pick your day and time, Step 4 of 6 | Book online | Trailhead Lawn & Irrigation"), so a screen reader hears the new question. (WCAG page titled and focus order.)
8. **Continue stays reachable.** On a phone Back and Continue are one bar fixed to the bottom of the screen, 48px tall, with the safe-area inset; on a laptop they are the pair at the card's foot. Continue is off only for a choice not made or a dead end, with a line saying why; otherwise a press names what is missing.
9. **Errors are summarised at the top of the step and the first bad field gets focus.** The summary lists each message in the same words as beside its field, each a button that focuses that field. (GOV.UK error summary; it focuses the summary, we focus the first field, because the field is where the fix is.)
10. **The booked screen has no Back and no Continue:** the card, what happens next, Add to calendar, "your Trailhead account" and "Back to the website". Nothing is left to go back to.

Layout that follows: the header is the logo (a link) at the left and Close at the right on a plain stone surface; the card sits at the top of a 640px column on a laptop (edge to edge on a phone with 16px gutters inside it); the ghosted photo is gone, because a faint photo made the card look unfinished and a photo strip would compete with the logo on a phone; the progress bar is in the card, so nothing is fixed at the top and nothing can sit under the walkthrough's Flow strip.

**What I fetched on Nov 21 2026, and what each said.**

- **GOV.UK Design System, [back link](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/back-link/)** (fetched): always include a back link on question pages; it takes people to the previous page "in the state they last saw it"; many people avoid the browser's Back because "some sites break when you use it", so the in-service link is the safer one; never use the back link together with the Breadcrumbs component.
- **GOV.UK Design System, [check answers](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/check-answers/)** (fetched): a "Change" link next to each section of the check answers page, with hidden text saying what it changes; after a change the Continue button returns the user to the check answers page; earlier answers are pre-filled; if a change needs new questions they are asked before returning; the page must say the transaction is not complete until the user confirms, and its button names the action.
- **NN/g, [wizards](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/wizards/)** (fetched): include buttons for the next and the previous step, named for what they do; show a list of steps with the current one; "enforce a clear sequential order"; if people must do steps in any order a wizard is the wrong pattern.
- **NN/g, [breadcrumbs](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/breadcrumbs/)** (fetched): breadcrumbs show where a page sits in a site's hierarchy, are not needed for a linear structure, and "are not intended to show the history of pages traversed during a session"; the page does not mention wizards or multi-step forms (that a progress trail or a summary of answers is a poor substitute is my inference, not NN/g's).
- **NN/g, [progress indicators](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progress-indicators/)** (fetched): show the number of steps, pair it with words, give it enough prominence.
- **Baymard, [average checkout form fields](https://baymard.com/blog/checkout-flow-average-form-fields)** (fetched): the only navigation-adjacent finding is that "usability does seem to suffer after 8+ checkout steps"; it says nothing about back links, Change links or a persistent summary. The Baymard usability benchmark page I fetched also says nothing about them.
- **Stripe Checkout** (fetched [How Checkout works](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/checkout/how-checkout-works) and [save and reuse](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/save-and-reuse?platform=checkout)): the pages describe the lifecycle (a session, a Stripe-hosted or embedded form, a return to the site) and, for saving a card without a charge, say to "add terms to your website or app that state how you plan to save payment method details and allow customers to opt in". They say nothing about a back link or a persistent summary on Stripe's page. **Unverified:** how Stripe's hosted page lets a customer go back.
- **Shopify checkout** (fetched [checkout settings](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/checkout-settings/customize-checkout-configurations)): the page does not describe a return link, an order summary or editing from a review step. **Unverified.**

**The rules adopted** (these replace the first pass's summary line and Change links; the first pass's rules 1 to 10 above still hold where they do not conflict):

1. **One way back: the Back button, beside Continue, and it is the browser's Back.** Nothing else on a step goes back: no link line over the question, no breadcrumb, no "Change" beside a part. Three ways to the same place made the form hard to read. (NN/g: explicit previous and next buttons. GOV.UK: never pair a back link with a second trail. The first pass keeps the button at the foot, where the thumb is on a phone, rather than GOV.UK's top-of-page link.)
2. **No summary of earlier answers over the steps.** The step count ("Step 3 of 6") says where you are; the answers are shown once, on Confirm. (Hannah; NN/g lists the steps and the current one, not the answers.) `BookingSummary` is deleted, and `FlowShell` has no summary slot.
3. **The only review is the Confirm step, with one Change link per row.** Each link is named for its row ("Change fall blowout") and goes to the step that asked. (GOV.UK check answers.)
4. **Change returns to Confirm.** A Change opens the step with the answer already in it, and Continue goes straight back to Confirm. The exception is the address and the service, which change what comes after them: they go on step by step with every answer kept. (GOV.UK: Continue returns to check answers; new questions come first.)
5. **Confirm says nothing is done until the button is pressed, and the button names the action:** "Confirm and book" for a visit, "Set up my Season Pass" for the pass, "Send to Ryan" for a request. (GOV.UK.)
6. **Back keeps the state the customer last saw.** Every answer lives in the form, never in a step's history entry, so Back, Forward and a Change never empty a field. (GOV.UK: "in the state they last saw it".)
7. **The step count is in words and is the count of the path:** "Step 3 of 6" for a repair, six or seven for a pass, four for a request. (NN/g progress indicators.) Baymard finds usability suffers after eight checkout steps.
8. **One heading per step, and it says what is being decided.** The preference step is "Your fall blowout", not "When would you like it?"; the card step is "Add a card". The page title carries the same words. (Hannah; GOV.UK: the title tells people what to do.)
9. **Close is always there and asks only once something is entered.** (First pass, rules 1 and 2.)
10. **A third party's form sits inside our step.** The card form is Stripe's own embedded form inside the "Add a card" step, so Back, the step count and Close stay ours and the customer never has two sets of navigation. This is a design choice, not something Stripe's pages say (unverified for Stripe's hosted page).
