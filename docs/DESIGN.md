# Design contract for the app surfaces

## What this is

These are the rules for every screen in `/admin`, the Season Pass checkout and the customer pass page. They are written for humans and for agents, so that a screen built in March looks like one built in November. The marketing site keeps its own rules in `CLAUDE.md`; this document only covers the app surfaces. A new block needs a story in Storybook and an entry here before it is used on a screen.

Status: as of 2026-11-12 the rails, thirteen blocks, six admin screens (Home, Passes, Revenue, Customers, Schedule, Settings; Schedule and Settings await Hannah), the six-step checkout and the customer pass page exist as Storybook stories on fixtures. Nothing is wired. Approval status per screen is in the Approvals section.

## Aesthetic direction

The feel is Stripe's dashboard: calm, light and numbers first, in Trailhead's palette. The canvas is cream, the sidebar is navy, and orange appears only on actions and highlights, never as decoration. Titles and big numbers are set in Barlow Condensed (`font-display`); everything else is Source Sans 3 (`font-sans`). Cards, buttons, panels and photos are `rounded-lg`. Circles are reserved for icon badges and chips.

Phase 1 is light mode only. Whitespace is generous; density comes from the data grid, not from the chrome around it. Every screen has one `PageHeader`, one main column of at most 1280px, and nothing competing with the numbers for attention.

### Spacing

Spacing uses Tailwind's default 4px grid and nothing outside it. The rail is 15rem (240px) on a laptop (`--sidebar-width`, set by AdminShell) and 3.5rem collapsed (`--sidebar-width-icon`, wide enough for the 40px mark with 8px either side). Across the content area runs a sticky 56px top bar: the sidebar trigger at the left, the page title (h1, `font-display`, 1.75rem) with its context line beside it on one baseline, and the page's actions at the right. `PageHeader` portals into that bar (`admin-bar.ts`); on a phone, where the bar holds the logo, and outside a shell it renders in the page. Under the bar the column is padded 24px on a laptop and 16px on a phone (`pt-6` / `pt-4`), `max-w-7xl` (1280px), left-aligned, never centred, so every screen has one 24px gap between the bar and its content. Bottom padding is `pb-8` (on a phone, `5rem` plus the safe-area inset, to clear the tab bar). Density: body text is `text-sm` (14px), lead lines 16px; data grid rows are 44px, the header 40px, the search field, filter chips and the Columns and Export buttons 36px on one row above the table. Stat tiles sit four across on a laptop and two across on a phone, and the two column layouts on Home stack on a phone. The sidebar collapses to icons at 1024px and becomes a bottom tab bar below 768px (767 and under, the same point where Tailwind's `md:` flips). AdminShell reads both breakpoints through `useSyncExternalStore` on the media queries, with a `false` server snapshot, and renders one tree at every width: the rail above 767px, the tab bar at or below it, and the inset around the screen always, so crossing a breakpoint never remounts the screen. The shadcn Sidebar primitive still uses `useIsMobile` internally for its own sheet; AdminShell does not render the rail on a phone, so that sheet never shows.

## Tokens

Every colour the app uses is a token in `src/app/globals.css`, inside the `@theme inline` block. The brand tokens came first and belong to the marketing site as well. The app tokens are mapped onto them; no new hue was invented. In Tailwind they are used as `bg-sidebar`, `text-positive`, `border-input`, `fill-chart-1` and so on. `@theme inline` inlines token values into the utilities that use them and only emits the `--color-x` custom property on `:root` when some generated CSS references it with `var()`. So `var(--color-chart-1)` in a chart config or inline style is undefined unless a class such as `fill-(--color-chart-1)` or `stroke-(--color-chart-1)` is also present; charts anchor their series that way.

### Type and radius

| Token | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `--font-sans` | Source Sans 3, then Segoe UI, Helvetica, Arial | Body, labels, table cells, buttons |
| `--font-display` | Barlow Condensed, then Arial Narrow, Impact | Page titles, section titles, big numbers in stat tiles and the pass card |
| `--radius` | `0.5rem` | The one corner radius, the same as Tailwind's `rounded-lg` |

Both faces are self hosted through `next/font` in `src/lib/fonts.ts`, which sets `--font-body-src` and `--font-display-src`. Storybook imports `globals.css` so stories render in the brand faces. There is no radius scale; `rounded-lg` is the radius for everything except circles. `--radius` is informational; `rounded-lg` resolves to Tailwind's own `--radius-lg`, which is the same 0.5rem. Never paste shadcn's base layer (`* { @apply border-border outline-ring/50 }`) into globals.css; it would change every bare border on the marketing site. Add primitives with `npx shadcn@latest add` only.

### The rule

In `src/components/blocks/` and `src/app/(admin)/`: only semantic tokens; no hex, no `rgb()`, no arbitrary-value colours, and no Tailwind palette colours (`red-500`, `amber-100`, and so on). `white` and `black` are allowed. In `src/components/ui/`: the same, except that files that predate this document may keep their palette colours until they are next touched. Marketing sections in `src/components/sections/` follow `CLAUDE.md`, not this rule.

If a colour is missing, add a token to `globals.css` and a row to the table above. `scripts/check-design.mjs` will fail the build once it lands (Task 0.4 of the prototype plan) when it finds a raw colour, a component without a story, or an import across the admin and marketing boundary.

## Three tiers

Components live in one of three folders, and each folder has one job.

`src/components/ui/` holds primitives generated by shadcn and adapted to our tokens. They are never edited ad hoc. A new primitive comes in with `npx shadcn@latest add <name>`, is checked against the tokens above, and gets a story. Button, badge, card, input, textarea, separator and carousel are already here.

`src/components/blocks/` holds composed pieces used by more than one screen: StatTile, SeasonLine, PassCard, PassSeason, Chart, DataGrid, PageHeader, EmptyState, StatusPill, NeedsAttention, SidePanel, RecordPanel, RecordFooter, ConfirmDialog, SplitButton, SegmentedControl, MoneyCell, FormRow, SectionHeading, WeekGrid, DayMap, ChangeLog, NotesAndHistory and the admin shell. The customer's account adds AccountNav, NextVisitCard, InvoiceBanner, VisitRow, JobRow, ReceiptLine and VisitReport ("What we did"; pure props, customer words from `src/lib/portal/words.ts`). A block takes data as props and knows nothing about where the data came from.

`src/app/(admin)/admin/_screens/`, and the `_screens/` folders under the checkout and account route groups, hold screens. A screen only composes blocks, takes its data as props and contains no fetching. Fetching happens in the route's `page.tsx` through `getAdminData()`, which is wired after the screen's story is approved.

Marketing sections stay in `src/components/sections/`. Nothing in the admin tree imports from marketing sections, and nothing in marketing imports from blocks or the admin tree. Admin code must never reach the marketing bundle.

Marketing CSS scans only the marketing folders and the seven primitives marketing uses (badge, button, card, carousel, input, separator, textarea). Tailwind cannot re-include one file under an excluded folder, so `globals.css` lists the admin-only primitives one by one as `@source not` lines instead; a primitive with no such line is in the marketing scan. A marketing file that imports one of the excluded primitives must remove its `@source not` line, and the gate fails the build until it does. Admin, checkout, account and booking screens are served by their own root layouts on `src/app/app.css`, which scans only the app folders; `globals.css` keeps its `@source not` lines so the two bundles never overlap. The gate also fails when marketing code (the `(site)` pages and `sections/`) imports a server library (`db`, `api`, `jobber-api`, `stripe`, `messaging`, `scheduling`, `geocode`, `pass/server`).

Files in blocks, ui and the app route groups stay under 300 lines; the gate fails above that. shadcn's sidebar and chart are allowlisted.

The reference libraries `ui/` and `pro-blocks/` at the repo root are read only and excluded from TypeScript. They are patterns to read and copy from, never imported.

## Storybook

Storybook is the catalog and the approval surface. Hannah approves a screen in Storybook, and only then is it wired to data.

Every file in `src/components/ui/` and `src/components/blocks/` has a sibling `*.stories.tsx`. Every block has `Default`, `Empty` and `Phone` stories; `Loading` (a skeleton) and `Error` (one line that says what to do) where the block owns async state. A pure presentational block that renders from props the screen already has needs no skeleton; the screen's `loading.tsx` or Suspense boundary covers it. Every screen has `Default`, `NoDataYet`, `Phone` and `Tablet`. Fixtures come from the anonymised Jobber snapshot so every story shows real shaped data.

`npm run storybook` opens the catalog on port 6006. `npm test` runs the unit project only; the story project needs a browser and runs separately through `npm run test:stories`.

The viewports are 375, 768, 1280 and 1440. The backgrounds are cream, white and navy. `npm run test:stories` renders every story in headless Chromium and fails on an accessibility violation, because `a11y.test` is set to `"error"` in `.storybook/preview.tsx`. An exception is scoped to one story, carries a comment that says why, and comes out when the cause is fixed.

## Tables

The DataGrid is built from shadcn's TanStack data table recipe and extended with column reorder and CSV export. These rules apply to every table in the app.

The first column is a human readable name, and it is the link that opens the record. Row click never fights with the selection checkbox: the name opens the record, the checkbox selects, and nothing else on the row is a target. Every column sorts. Filters are visible chips with an active state, so what is filtering the table is never hidden in a menu. Columns can be hidden and reordered from a menu. The header is sticky. The bulk action bar appears above the grid only when rows are selected, and goes away when the selection is cleared. CSV export writes every row that matches the current search and filters, in the visible columns, in the current sort, and the button says how many rows that is. An empty result goes through `EmptyState`, with a line that says what to do next.

From `md` up the table keeps its sticky header, frozen first column and sideways scroll with a cut off edge as the cue; columns can be hidden to make room. Below `md` a screen's grid is one card per row (`DataGridCard`, the screen's `card` mapping: name, a quiet line, the status pill, the day, the money word at the right, the whole card opens the record), so no list is ever a table that only works sideways; search, filters, count and empty states are the same on both. A grid with no `card` mapping stays the sideways table.

Record detail opens in a side panel, one component (`SidePanel`) the same on every screen, built on the shadcn `Sheet` (modal dialog: scrim, focus kept in and returned, Escape) and `Resizable` (react-resizable-panels). On a laptop it floats over the page from the right, full height, with a translucent scrim that closes it on click; nothing under it moves, so the grid keeps its columns. A handle on its left edge resizes it between 360px and 70% of the window (drag, or the left and right arrow keys in 40px steps, named "Resize panel") and the width is remembered per browser. On a phone it is a full width sheet with no handle. A grid that is wider than its box scrolls inside its own container (never the page) with the name column frozen. Every admin list is a `DataGrid` with the shared column definitions: Visits and the Schedule's day list use `visitColumns` (the day list without Day), Customers and Jobs their own; no screen writes table markup or column widths for a list of records (the Settings editors and the Schedule's week calendar are not lists of records).

## Uniformity

Every record panel (Customer, Job, Visit) is one block, `RecordPanel` (on `SidePanel`), with the existing tokens and type only (Barlow Condensed headings, Source Sans 3 body, navy, cream and the orange accent; no new colours). A panel passes data, never markup; the parts are in "The recipe" below.

- **Header.** The name in Barlow Condensed 24px navy, one muted subtitle line under it that never repeats a pill (the Visit panel reads "<Service>, <day>"; the Customer panel the street and town; the Job panel "Repair · sold Nov 3" or "Season Pass TH-2027-0007 · 2027"), the pills under the subtitle (`StatusPill`, `StatusPills`), and one Close on the right. 24px side padding, 16px top and bottom, one rule under it.
- **Facts.** Directly under the header, no heading: a two column list with the label column fixed at 112px (`RecordFacts`: muted label, medium value). Visit: When, Who, Minutes, Price, Job (a link up), Phone (tap to call). Job: Customer (a link), Total, Unpaid, Next. Customer: Phone, Email, Job, Next, Scheduled, Marketing, Card.
- **Body.** 24px side padding and 16px top and bottom, one column with 24px between sections. A section is `SectionHeading` (Barlow Condensed 18px navy, a quiet Edit or Add at its right) then its rows, 8px below it. The body scrolls on its own and is a named region. No filled button and no form grows in it.
- **Rows.** `RecordRowText` on the left (a title over a muted detail, each one line with an ellipsis and the full words in its title) and one pill or amount on the right (`RecordRowEnd`, never shrinks); every row 44px tall at least with 6px of padding, a hairline between rows (`RecordList`). Nothing runs past the panel's edge.
- **Footer.** `RecordFooter`: one bar under the body with a rule over it and the same padding. Left to right one filled primary, up to two outlines, a More menu and one destructive ghost at the far right. A button acts at once with ten seconds of Undo, or opens one dialog (`ConfirmDialog`); nothing in the footer grows a form. On a phone the primary has its own row. Which buttons a record has in each state is its footer plan (`visitBar`, `jobBar`, `CustomerFooter`; the tables are in `docs/plans/2026-11-18-admin-holistic-design.md`, section 7). A tech sees the buttons their role allows and no others. A record with no buttons (a Done and paid job, a canceled one, a tech) has no footer strip. The Job panel keeps the audit's rule that a Done and paid job has no primary (New visit is under More) and Collect leads a done job that is unpaid; section 7's table differs on those rows (New visit as the primary, Send an invoice and Remind as buttons), which waits for Hannah's call.
- **Beat two.** After Mark done the Visit panel itself turns to "How is this paid?": `CollectPayment` is the body and its one button goes to the footer (`RecordFooter slot`), with a Cancel until the money has gone through. Undo done and Refund are under More.
- **Pills.** Under the subtitle in the header, or at the right of a row; never in a grid cell.
- **One panel at a time.** A screen has one record panel open: opening another record (a visit from a job, a job from the New job sheet) closes the first, never stacks a second over it. The Job panel's footer leads with the next step for the job (Add a line, Open visit, Collect $145); a Done and paid job has no primary, only New visit under More.

## Navigation

The left column is the whole app: Home, Schedule, Jobs, Customers, Reports, Settings, and the lit item follows the address on every page (`AdminShell` reads `usePathname()`). **Visits is not in it** (Hannah, Nov 18 2026, step 2 of the holistic design): a visit is one trip to the house, and the word names that trip anywhere (Today's visits, Last visit, a Visits column); what the gate forbids is a Visits nav item, a `visits` section or a link to `/admin/visits`. The List is the Schedule's third view beside Week and Day (`SegmentedControl`, `?view=list`), the old Visits grid with the same columns and filters; it can be filtered to one job (`?forJob=`, with "Show all" over the grid); `/admin/visits` redirects to it, keeping `?visit=` and the filters. On a phone the bottom bar is Home, Schedule, Jobs, Customers and More, whose menu holds Reports, Settings and Sign out. **The address holds the open record**: `?customer=`, `?job=` or `?visit=` on every screen (Home, Schedule, Jobs and Customers wrap themselves in `RecordHost`), one panel at a time over the screen you are on; opening pushes an entry, so Back returns to the last record, Close returns to the page, a shared link opens the record, and a record opened from another shows "‹ Andrea Reyes" above its title. **New job** is the one filled button in the header of Home, Schedule, Jobs and Customers; on the Schedule the Day view's header and each week column's "+" open it with that day filled in. **Cmd or Ctrl+K**, or the Search button, finds customers, jobs and days by name, street, phone or pass number and opens the record in place. Every link, button and row target lands on one of those pages with the record found there: a job opens its panel on Jobs (`?job=`, "Open job"), a visit its panel on the Schedule (`?visit=`, on the week it is in), a customer on Customers (`?customer=`), a day on Schedule (`?day=`). The hierarchy is customer, then job, then visit: a visit panel names its job on one line under the pills ("Season Pass TH-2027-0007 · 2027" or "Repair · sold Nov 3") and links up to it, the job panel lists its visits, and the customer panel lists its jobs, each opening to its visits. Nothing makes a visit without a job: "New visit" on a job panel adds one to that job (`visits.create` with the job's id) and New job is the only way to make a job. `/admin/passes` redirects: `?pass=<id>` to that pass's job on Jobs, alone to Jobs filtered to Season Passes. The allow-list lives in `ADMIN_ROUTES` (`src/lib/admin/links.ts`) and `nav-audit.test.tsx` fails when any screen, with its panel open, links outside it. The customer panel names the pass once as a "Job" row ("Season Pass TH-2027-0007 · 2027 · Active", linking to that job), shows the Season Pass view (`PassSeason`) under the customer's facts with earlier seasons folded below it ("2026 season"), and lists the customer's jobs.

## Charts

There are two chart shapes and no others: grouped bars by month, with this year in `chart-1` and last year in `chart-2` drawn dashed, and a single line. No pies, donuts, gauges, treemaps or 3D.

Colour highlights; it never encodes magnitude. A bar is orange because it is this year, not because it is big. Tooltips carry the exact numbers, formatted the same way as the tiles.

Stat tiles always name their comparison baseline in words, for example "vs October 2025", never with a bare arrow. The delta is coloured by whether the movement is good for the business, `positive` or `warning`, not by whether the number went up or down. More past due invoices is up and bad; fewer is down and good.

## Copy

The app follows the same copy rules as the site. Headings are sentence case. Sentences are short and complete; no fragments, no "Yes!", no em dashes. Booking buttons say "Book Online". Money is whole dollars with a comma, as in `$1,234`. Dates are written out where there is room ("October 14") and as a short form in table cells ("Oct 14"). Status words are plain: paid, past due, confirmed, canceled.
